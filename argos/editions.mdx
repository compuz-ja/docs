ARGOS comes in four editions. Each one answers a different kind of question, and a report belongs to exactly one.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Comply, tier 1: what happened?">
    Descriptive. Statements of position, registers of what occurred, and everything a regulator, an auditor or the board compels the institution to produce. Forty reports.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Understand, tier 2: why did it happen?">
    Diagnostic. Value comes from a breakdown or a comparison: delinquency by branch and product, budget against actual, growth by family. Eighteen reports.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Anticipate, tier 3: what happens next?">
    Predictive. The output estimates something not yet observed. Every report at this tier carries a disclaimer that travels into every export. Four reports.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Decide, tier 4: what should we do?">
    Prescriptive. The output ranks options or recommends an action, and records what a named officer decided about it. Four reports.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What decides a report's edition

Seven rules, in order. The first that matches wins.

| | Rule | Edition |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Compelled by a regulator, an auditor or the board | Comply |
| 2 | Evidence that the numbers are sound | Comply |
| 3 | A statement of position, or a register of what happened | Comply |
| 4 | Value comes from a breakdown or a comparison | Understand |
| 5 | The output estimates something not yet observed | Anticipate |
| 6 | The output ranks options or recommends an action | Decide |

Rule 1 beats the predictive rule, and that ordering is deliberate. The IFRS 9 expected credit loss figure is forward-looking and still sits at Comply, because a credit union is compelled to publish it. **The edition follows the job, not the mathematics.**

## What a locked report looks like

A report your edition does not cover is listed, never hidden. You see that it exists, what it answers and which edition opens it. The same applies to drill-down: an edition that does not include a breakdown still shows you the shape of the answer, which is how many components the figure has and how far the largest of them moved.

## Decide is different

A recommendation is not a decision. Nothing in ARGOS acts on a recommendation automatically, and a recommendation cannot leave the open state without a named officer recorded against it, with the evidence it rested on written down at the time.

That is a constraint in the database rather than a convention in the interface, and it exists because section 12 of Jamaica's Data Protection Act 2020 restricts decisions taken solely by automated means where they produce legal or similarly significant effects on a person.
