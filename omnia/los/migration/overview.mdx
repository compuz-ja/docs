Loan migration imports your credit union's existing loans, each recorded just as it was originally made and placed at the stage it had really reached. A member's file, the officers' decisions and the loan's position all live in one place from day one.

It is a one-time import of loans already made, not a new origination. The rate, risk band, approval outcome and disbursement figures are stored exactly as a person set them on paper. Nothing is recalculated, so a migrated figure is what someone decided, not what the system would work out today.

<Note>
  Loan migration imports **loans**. To import **member** records, use [Bulk import](/omnia/los/members/bulk-import). Bring members in first so each migrated loan can be matched to its borrower.
</Note>

## What is out of scope

Servicing, repayment schedules and running balances. The Loan Origination System covers origination, so a migrated loan carries its origination history and final figures, not its repayment record.

## Two ways in

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Bulk workbook import" icon="file-spreadsheet">
    Upload a spreadsheet of your loans, one row per loan, with linked sheets for guarantors, collateral and disbursement payees. Best for a whole book. See [The migration workbook](/omnia/los/migration/workbook).
  </Card>
  <Card title="Guided single-loan entry" icon="pen-line">
    Key one paper loan at a time and attach a photo or scan of the paper file. Best for one-off files. The scan is filed as the source record; it is not read automatically.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

Both are under **Administration → Loan Migration**.

## Run a batch

Every import, bulk or guided, becomes a **batch** on the migrations console, showing its status, counts (staged, committed, errored), who uploaded it and who signed it off. Open a batch to work through it.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Upload and stage">
    Click **Upload a batch** and drop in your workbook. Say whether amounts are in dollars or whole cents, and enter the control totals you expect: the number of loans and their total principal. The file is virus-scanned, every row is read, and each loan is staged for review. Nothing becomes a live loan yet.

    Each loan comes back **Ready**, **Needs review** or **Errored**. One bad row never fails the batch: errored rows go into a downloadable report you fix and re-upload.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review and attribute each loan">
    Confirm the borrower for each loan, and set who handled it at each stage: the officer who captured it, the approver who decided it, the disbursement officer who released it. Amounts and dates are shown back in plain form, like `1,250,000.00` and `14 Mar 2021`, so a scale or date mistake is caught before anything commits.

    Attribution saves as you go, so you can stop and come back, and two reviewers can work the same batch. To set one officer across many loans, tick the rows, pick the officer and click **Apply to selected**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Reconcile (dry-run)">
    Click **Reconcile (dry-run)** to preview the count and total the batch will import against your control totals. Nothing is committed. With control totals entered, the result is clean only when count and principal match exactly. Without them, it is a summary, not a pass or fail.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Sign off (optional)">
    Click **Sign off** to record an approver on the batch. It is not required to import. See [Sign-off is optional](#sign-off-is-optional).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Commit">
    Click **Commit** to turn the ready loans into real loans: all of them by default, or only the ones you tick. Loans still needing review stay staged for later. A large book commits in chunks, and a run that stops part way resumes where it left off. Committing a loan that is already in does nothing, so it is always safe to run again.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Generate a compliance attestation">
    After commit, click **Generate attestation** for a signed record of exactly what came in. It appears in the batch's **Compliance attestations** panel with a **Download** button.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Every loan lands at its true stage

A migrated loan is placed at the stage it had really reached, not reset to draft. Your book is a mix: some **Disbursed**, some **Filed**, some **Declined**, some still in **Credit approval**. You set the stage per loan, with the historical dates behind it.

Because the stage and dates are real, reports count a migrated loan in its true year. A loan disbursed in 2021 counts in 2021, not in the month you imported it.

## Former staff, attributed without a login

The person who approved a loan five years ago may have left. You still credit the work to them.

A former officer who is not a user is recorded as a **historical actor**: a named person with no sign-in. They appear in the attribution picker and in reports under their own name, but never in the assignment picker, and they cannot sign in. When two people share a name, a legacy employee ID tells them apart.

## Identity checks for migrated borrowers

A borrower created during migration has their identity recorded from the paper evidence, never from a live check. With an ID scan on file they are **Verified**. Without one, or when your credit union requires fresh checks, they go to **Manual review**.

## Reconciliation

Reconciliation checks that the number of loans and total value going in match what you expected. You enter the expected count and principal at upload. The console compares them with what staged, and after commit with what landed. With no expected totals, you still see the staged and committed summary as information.

## Sign-off is optional

Yes, you can commit without a sign-off. Anyone with migration access can commit the ready loans directly.

The person who uploads a batch can also commit it. Two-person control belongs on live disbursement, where money moves, not on migration, where you are recording loans already made. See [Disbursement controls](/omnia/los/disbursement/controls).

Sign-off is there when your own procedure calls for a second set of eyes. It records a named approver on the batch, and when you record one, a clean reconciliation is required first.

## Commit all, or only the loans you tick

Commit imports every ready loan by default. Tick loans first to import exactly those and leave the rest staged. Bring in the clean loans now and finish the rest in a later pass.

## The compliance attestation

An attestation is a tamper-evident, signed record of exactly what a batch imported: how many loans, their total value, whether the control totals reconciled, and who uploaded and signed off. It is your proof of the import for auditors and your regulator.

It is kept in storage that cannot be edited or deleted for your retention period, and anyone can verify it independently without trusting the screen. Every attestation is timestamped and kept in the **Compliance attestations** panel, each with **Download**. Generate a fresh one at any time; earlier ones stay on file.

## Voiding a batch

If a batch was imported in error, void the whole batch at once. Voiding needs a reason and marks every loan in the batch as voided.

A voided loan is never deleted. It stays on record but drops out of queues, dashboards and analytics. The audit trail export still lists it, marked voided. To correct a mis-imported batch, void it and import a corrected one.

## Who can run migrations

| Permission | What it allows |
|---|---|
| **Import loans** | Upload a workbook and use guided single-loan entry. |
| **Review loan imports** | Review staged loans, match borrowers, attribute officers and reconcile. |
| **Sign off loan imports** | Sign off, commit, void and generate the attestation. |

The Administrator, Credit Manager, Credit Officer and Branch Manager hold all three. The Adjudicator, Securities and Disbursement roles hold none. See [Roles and permissions](/omnia/los/access/roles-and-permissions).

Every step, from upload to void, is recorded in the [audit trail](/omnia/los/compliance/audit-trail) with who did it and when, as is every borrower and historical actor created along the way.
