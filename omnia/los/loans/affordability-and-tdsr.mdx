The TDSR (total debt service ratio) is the share of a member's counted monthly income that goes to monthly outgoings once the new loan is added. The Credit Officer sees it live in the **Income & expenses** section while capturing, and the same figure is recorded when the application is submitted. The number on the capture screen is the number the decision is made on.

## The formula

Three inputs, all monthly:

- **Counted income.** Each income line multiplied by the share of it that counts.
- **Expenses after this loan.** Every expense at its after-loan amount. A debt this loan pays off counts as zero.
- **This loan's monthly payment.** The instalment plus any compulsory savings and the monthly creditor life amount.

```latex
\text{TDSR} = \frac{\text{expenses after this loan} + \text{this loan's monthly payment}}{\text{counted income}}
```

**Money left over** (residual income) uses the same numbers subtracted instead of divided: counted income minus expenses after this loan minus this loan's monthly payment. A negative figure shows in red.

## Income lines and the share that counts

Not every dollar of income counts in full. Each income type counts at a share your credit union sets: for example, allowances might count at 75% while basic salary counts in full. The share follows the income type you pick, so you cannot change it on a line.

The income types are **Basic Salary (gross)**, **Allowances**, **Performance-Based Pay**, **Sole Trader**, **Partner**, **Existing Real Estate Rental**, **Projected Real Estate Rental** and **Other Income**. Put commission, overtime and bonuses on a **Performance-Based Pay** line, not under Basic Salary, or they count at the salary share.

Under each line the screen shows the share and the counted amount, for example **75% counts · 30,000.00**. **Total income** on the totals strip is the counted figure, with the gross underneath when the two differ.

## Expenses: current and projected

List every monthly outgoing, not only debts: statutory deductions, pension, existing loan payments, property costs, utilities, transport, personal and family costs. The categories are **Statutory Deductions**, **Pension**, **Existing Loan Payment**, **Other Payment**, **Property-Related**, **Utility / Household**, **Personal / Family**, **Transportation** and **Other Expense**. **Quick add** chips add the common ones in one click.

An **Existing Loan Payment** or **Other Payment** line has a second field, **After this loan**, for the projected amount when the new loan consolidates, refinances or pays off that debt.

- Leave it blank and the line counts at its current amount.
- Click **Paid off** to set it to zero. The line reads "paid off in full".
- Type a smaller figure for a partial consolidation. The line reads "reduced from" the current amount.

When any line changes, a green panel appears: **This loan lowers existing debt by** the monthly difference, with **Before this loan** and **After this loan** figures for the TDSR and the money left over. The after figure is the one that counts.

## This loan's monthly payment

The new payment appears as its own line, **This loan's monthly payment**, beneath the expenses. You do not type it. A breakdown under the totals shows its parts:

| Part | How it is worked out |
|---|---|
| **Instalment** | Principal and interest over the term at the rate the loan will be priced at on approval. |
| **Compulsory savings** | A share of the instalment, never less than the minimum set on the [loan product](/omnia/los/admin/loan-products). The same figure prints on the agreement. |
| **Creditor life** | Paid **Annually**: the annual premium divided by 12, so a yearly cost is not invisible to affordability. Paid **In full**, no cover, or a cash-secured loan: zero. |

The rate is a manual override when one is in force, otherwise the rate for the loan's risk band and collateral. When the loan cannot be priced yet, usually because the collateral is not captured, the breakdown uses the product's default rate and says **(product default rate)**.

Creditor life paid annually or in full is also collected once at disbursement as a fee, not as part of the monthly payment. See [Fees and creditor life at capture](/omnia/los/loans/fees-and-insurance-at-capture).

## The TDSR chip

The **TDSR** tile on the totals strip shows the percentage and a coloured chip: **Healthy** (green), **Elevated** (amber) or **Exceeds policy** (red) as the ratio crosses the policy thresholds. With no income captured the tile shows a dash and **No income captured**.

A high TDSR does not block submission. It is flagged for the approver, who decides.

## Worked example

<Note>
  Illustrative round figures only, not any credit union's policy or rates. The shares and rates are examples.
</Note>

A member applies for a loan that pays off an existing car loan. Allowances count at 75% in this example.

| Income line | Amount | Share | Counted |
|---|---|---|---|
| Basic Salary (gross) | 220,000 | 100% | 220,000 |
| Allowances | 40,000 | 75% | 30,000 |
| **Total** | **260,000** | | **250,000** |

| Expense line | Current | After this loan |
|---|---|---|
| Statutory Deductions | 40,000 | 40,000 |
| Utility / Household | 20,000 | 20,000 |
| Existing Loan Payment | 30,000 | 0 (Paid off) |
| Transportation | 10,000 | 10,000 |
| **Total** | **100,000** | **70,000** |

| This loan's monthly payment | Amount |
|---|---|
| Instalment | 30,000 |
| Compulsory savings | 1,500 |
| Creditor life (12,000 a year ÷ 12) | 1,000 |
| **Total** | **32,500** |

The result:

- **TDSR** = (70,000 + 32,500) ÷ 250,000 = **41.0%**.
- **Money left over** = 250,000 − 70,000 − 32,500 = **147,500**.
- **Before this loan**: 100,000 ÷ 250,000 = 40.0%, with 150,000 left over.

Had the member paid creditor life in full, the monthly payment would be 31,500 and the TDSR 40.6%.

## Risk band, credit rating and the Risk Registry

The **risk band** is a score built from the application's factors (employment, TDSR, loan-to-value, loan type, credit history), grouped into five bands from **R1** (lowest risk) to **R5** (highest). It is assessed at submission and again on every resubmission.

The **Credit rating & risk registry** section captures what the officer finds outside the system:

- **Rating**: **Very low risk** to **Very high risk**, from the member's credit report. It is the credit history part of the score. **Not captured** counts as average risk. **High risk** and **Very high risk** also flag the loan for the approver.
- **Credit score (optional)**: a whole number from 0 to 2000, for the record. It does not change the band.
- **Credit report source (optional)**: the bureau or report reference, up to 120 characters.
- **Risk Registry check**: **Clear**, **Adverse listing found** or **Not checked**. An adverse listing shows in red on the approval page. It does not change the band.

The section is editable while the application is a **Draft**. A change takes effect at the next submission.

<Note>
  The application form does not capture the applicant's employment details yet, so the employment part of the score uses standard values at submission. Read the band with that in mind. The member's employment evidence is in their documents.
</Note>

After submission the **Risk rating** section on the loan, and the **Risk assessment** card on the approval page, show the band, any flags and a **How the score was reached** breakdown.

## Frequently asked questions

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Why does my TDSR differ from our spreadsheet?">
    Check five things in order. The counted share: income that counts at less than 100% raises the TDSR against a sheet that counts everything in full. The new payment: it includes compulsory savings and monthly creditor life, not just the instalment. Expenses: every outgoing counts, not only debts. Paid-off debts: they count at the after-loan amount. The rate: compare the rate in the breakdown with your sheet's, since the screen prices at the rate approval will use.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Does the TDSR change between the capture screen and submission?">
    No. Both use the same calculation and inputs. Change the amount, term, creditor life choice or rate override and the screen recalculates, and submission records the new figure.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can a member with a high TDSR be approved?">
    Yes, if the approver decides so. A TDSR above policy is flagged and raises the risk score, but it does not block submission.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Why is creditor life in my TDSR when nothing is charged monthly?">
    With **Annually**, the annual premium divided by 12 counts so a yearly cost is not invisible. Nothing is charged monthly: the first year's premium is collected at disbursement. With **In full**, creditor life adds nothing to the TDSR.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="The breakdown says product default rate. What does that mean?">
    The loan cannot be priced yet, usually because its collateral is not captured. Capture the collateral and the breakdown switches to the loan's own rate.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can I change an income line's share?">
    No. The share follows the income type. Pick the type that matches the income.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Where does residual income show?">
    As **Money left over** on the totals strip, and in the before and after panel when a debt is being paid off.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
