**Change loan type / product** moves a draft application to a different product or kind of loan without abandoning it and re-keying everything. What happens depends on whether the new choice is the same kind of loan.

| You pick | What happens | Loan number |
|---|---|---|
| Another product of the **same kind** (for example one Unsecured product to another) | The draft is changed in place. | Kept |
| A product of a **different kind** (for example Unsecured to Motor vehicle) | A new draft is created and the capture is copied across. The old draft is abandoned. | New |

The kind of loan is Unsecured, Cash secured, Motor vehicle or Real estate. The loan number includes the loan type code, for example `MAIN-UNS-00001`, which is why a change of kind needs a new number.

## Who can do it

- **Same kind**: **Edit loans**.
- **Different kind**: **Edit loans** and **Create loans**. Without **Create loans**, the dialog lists only products of the same kind and says: "Moving to a different kind of loan creates a new application, which your role cannot do."

The Credit Officer holds both. The application must be a **Draft**. A loan returned for more information can be changed once it is back in **Draft**.

## Change the product or type

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the draft">
    **Change loan type / product** is in the header of the application page.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick the loan type and product">
    Choose a **loan type**, then a **product** under it. The list shows each product's name and code.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Read what will happen">
    For the same kind, the dialog says the loan number is kept and lists what is cleared. For a different kind, it says a new draft with a new loan number will be created and the current one abandoned, and lists what carries over and what does not.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm">
    Click **Change product** (same kind) or **Create new draft** (different kind). A different kind takes you to the new draft with the message "New draft NEW-ID created. OLD-ID was abandoned."
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Same kind: changed in place

The loan keeps its number, history and capture. Anything priced against the old product is cleared:

- the rate override
- the fee treatment choices
- the escrow months override
- the product-limit exception reason
- optional document requirements that belonged to the old loan type, when the new product sits under a different loan type

Estimates recalculate for the new product. If the amount or term is outside the new product's limits, you get a warning rather than a refusal, so you are not trapped on the old product. Fix the amount or term before submitting: saving the loan parameters and approval both enforce the limits. The change is recorded in the audit trail.

## Different kind: a new draft

The new draft gets a new loan number. Copied across:

- the member, branch, requested amount, term and purpose
- the transaction type and any balance being paid off
- income and expense lines, with their after-loan amounts
- net worth entries
- employment records
- active guarantors and their employment details
- the credit rating, credit score, report source and Risk Registry check
- the creditor life settings, when cover can apply to the new kind
- the current assignee

Not copied (the dialog lists these before you confirm):

- collateral and security records
- prepared or signed legal documents and draft agreements
- security requests, holds and security documents
- guarantor documents (they stay on the abandoned draft; upload them again)
- guarantor acknowledgements
- the rate override, fee treatment choices, custom fee lines and escrow months override
- the product-limit exception reason
- the Credit Officer recommendation and approval level
- optional document requirements

Creditor life follows the cash-secured rule. Moving to a cash-secured loan turns cover off. Moving from one gives the new draft the default cover settings.

The old draft becomes **Draft abandoned** with the reason "Re-typed to NEW-ID". It is never deleted, and the audit trail links the two records. The change happens all at once, so you never end up with two live drafts or none.

## When a change of kind is refused

- **A migrated loan** cannot be re-typed: "A migrated loan cannot be re-typed."
- **An application from the online channel** cannot change kind, because the member's online application is linked to it. You can still change the product within the same kind.

## Frequently asked questions

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Do I lose the income and expenses I captured?">
    No. Income, expenses, net worth, employment and guarantors carry across either way.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Why did my loan number change?">
    You moved to a different kind of loan. The number includes the loan type code, so the new draft takes the next number for that type. The old number stays on the abandoned draft.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can I change the type after submitting?">
    No. Only a **Draft** can be changed. If the application was returned for more information, change it once it is back in **Draft**.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="I changed to a car loan. Why is the collateral section empty?">
    Collateral never carries to a new kind of loan, because the old security does not fit. Capture the vehicle on the new draft.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="The new product's maximum is lower than my amount. Is the change refused?">
    No. You get a warning and the change goes through. Lower the amount, or record a product-limit exception if your credit union allows one, before you submit.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Where do the guarantor documents go?">
    They stay on the abandoned draft. Upload them again on the new draft's guarantors.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
