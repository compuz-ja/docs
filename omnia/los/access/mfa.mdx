Multi-factor authentication (MFA) adds a 6-digit code from an authenticator app to your sign-in. Any standard authenticator app works: Google Authenticator, Microsoft Authenticator, Authy, 1Password, and others. Once it is on, every sign-in asks for a fresh code, unless you trusted the device at an earlier sign-in.

<Tip>
  Turn on MFA for every account. It matters most for **Administrators** and anyone who approves loans, disburses funds, or changes roles, because those accounts can move money and grant access.
</Tip>

## Turning on MFA

Install an authenticator app on your phone or computer first.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the Authentication page">
    Click your avatar in the top-right corner and select **Authentication**. You can also open **Profile** and pick **Authentication** in the profile sidebar.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Click Enable">
    The **Two-factor authentication** row shows an **INACTIVE** badge and an **Enable** button. Click **Enable**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm your password">
    Type your current password and click **Continue**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Scan the QR code">
    In your authenticator app, add an account and scan the QR code. The app starts showing a new 6-digit code every 30 seconds. If you cannot scan, click **Can't scan? Enter the key manually** and type the key into the app.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enter the 6-digit code">
    Type the current code into the six boxes. It checks itself the moment you enter the sixth digit; there is no submit button. Pasting a copied code fills all six boxes at once. A wrong code clears the boxes so you can try again.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save your backup codes">
    You get 10 one-time backup codes. Use **Copy all** or **Download** and keep them somewhere safe, such as a password manager or a printed sheet in a locked drawer. Click **I've saved my codes** to finish.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
  Backup codes are shown once. Save all 10 before you finish. They are your only self-service way back in if you lose your authenticator app.
</Warning>

## Signing in with MFA

<Steps>
  <Step title="Enter your email and password">
    Sign in as usual.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enter the code from your app">
    Type the current 6-digit code into the six boxes. Codes last 30 seconds. If yours is about to change, wait for the next one.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Trust the device if only you use it">
    Tick **Trust this device for 30 days** before you verify, and that device skips the code for 30 days. Leave it unticked on shared or public computers.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Use a backup code if you need to">
    Without your app, click **Use a backup code** and enter one of the codes you saved.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Lost your authenticator app

Click **Use a backup code** on the verification screen and enter one of your saved codes. Each works once. Once you are in, open **Authentication** and set up your new app.

<Warning>
  If you have lost your app **and** used up or lost your backup codes, you cannot get back in on your own, and your administrator cannot reset MFA for you. Contact your LOS support team to recover the account.
</Warning>

## Managing MFA after setup

When MFA is on, the **Two-factor authentication** row shows an **ENABLED** badge and a **Manage** button. There is no button to turn MFA off.

<Warning>
  **Manage** starts setup again from scratch: a password check, a new QR code, and 10 new backup codes. Finishing it cancels your old backup codes and the old entry in your authenticator app. Use it only when you mean to start over, for example on a new phone.
</Warning>
